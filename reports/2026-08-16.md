## INSIGHTS
The 3 most important insights. For each:
- **Title** — one sentence on why it matters to a solo builder shipping AI

- **Title** — Rigid micromanagement hobbles modern AI models; solo builders must
transition to high-level governance by supplying robust business context and 
allowing autonomous execution.
  Traditional prompt engineering often micromanages AI with overly specific, 
step-by-step instructions [1]. However, modern frontier models perform 
significantly better when given a high-level goal, robust context (such as 
files, goals, and calendars), and the freedom to execute independently [1-3]. By
unhobbling the model and utilizing flexible, goal-oriented prompts like "do 
smart things," solo builders can shift their role from basic task delegators to 
strategic overseers [2, 4, 5].

- **Title** — AI cannot grade its own homework; solo builders must utilize 
multi-agent 'gauntlet loops' with fresh-context critics to ensure 
production-grade outputs.
  When a single AI model is asked to review its own output, it suffers from a 
lack of objective evaluation, often confidently validating its own mistakes [6, 
7]. To overcome this limitation, solo builders must implement a "gauntlet loop" 
where fresh-context critics are dynamically spun up to ruthlessly critique the 
main model's work across dimensions like visual impact and brief compliance 
[7-9]. Running these automated, multi-round critiques dramatically elevates 
design and code quality in a single turn [7, 10].

- **Title** — Monolithic 'mega-agents' break under complex tasks; solo builders 
should construct specialized, role-driven teams with narrow scopes that delegate
to each other.
  Giving a single agent a broad, vague job description leads to confusion, 
bloated context windows, and eventual failure [11-13]. Instead, builders should 
design an "AI org chart" consisting of narrow, specialized agents with distinct 
roles, allowing them to read each other's descriptions and autonomously delegate
sub-tasks [11, 14-16].
